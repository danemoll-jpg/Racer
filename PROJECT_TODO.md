### Part A — Hitting a bumper or barrier turns you back along it instead of stopping you (Chastain Round 85)
1. **HIGH MODE bumpers** (`HighBumpers`): a hit at any angle short of head-on turns you back along the course and keeps your speed (a glancing hit loses little; the harder the angle the more it costs), and you leave pointed down the course, never stopped, spun or riding along it. If you are pinned slow against a bumper (Chastain: 0.6 s), you are turned down the course and set going (Chastain: 8 m/s). Smooth continuous colliders, no posts, seams or corners to catch on, no friction on the vehicle's side, the push sideways, never up (no climbing, no launch).
2. **Every barrier in every mode**, the same way: race closure barriers and barricades (`StuntShortcuts`, Leviathan's `Block`s, review7's new barrier at the end of Leviathan Reverse's mountain pass), ROAD CLOSED fences, the mountain-track guardrails and natural barriers, the Free Roam blocked-road barricades, bridge rails and other guide walls. A glancing hit slides you along and straightens you to its line; a **head-on hit in normal mode is still a crash** (Chastain: glance up to 50° in normal, 82° in HIGH; past that, a crash for the rest of that touch). Breakable things (fences meant to break, signs, mailboxes, smash spots, breakable trees) stay breakable; houses and trees are not part of this. Say which colliders were changed.

4. Take Chastain's numbers as the starting point (speed kept `1 − (1 − cos a) × 0.8`, HIGH 0.6; held on the wall's line 0.18 s; the angle limits above); say if anything had to differ here.

2. Where a bumper starts or stops (at a jump, a shortcut opening or anywhere else), its end is **rounded and angled in**, so hitting it turns you back onto the course (Part A); never a square end you can catch.
3. The same room for the mountain-track guardrails and barriers in normal mode at the lips and landings of jumps, wherever one stands in a flight or landing path today (list them; move or shorten each so it is clear).
